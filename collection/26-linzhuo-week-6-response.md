*Responding to Jonathan Lee. Linzhuo’s note on what was said:* He discussed how to incorporate AI into our workflow and what mindset to adopt in the face of the rapidly evolving AI industry.

> I found his talk very inspiring, especially the sections on GitHub and Worktrees. I learned a lot of new ways of thinking and interesting perspectives. In the Q&A before the session, I asked him, as a designer, how we should approach the increasingly powerful AI and to what extent we should use it—and whether the effort we put into learning a new software tool might soon be replaced by AI. Design and art are arguably among the first fields to be impacted by the rise of AI. As visual professionals, our aesthetic sensibilities and creative ideas are crucial, yet AI is devouring these distinct styles. By “piecing together” images one by one to blend them seamlessly and treating design concepts as templates, AI produces a vast number of decent visual effects with minimal effort. This is undoubtedly frustrating and confusing for designers. In his talk, Jonathan mentioned that we should “have fun with it.” Rather than constantly fearing that our jobs will be replaced, we should try to use these tools to achieve things we couldn’t do before. Everything has its pros and cons; as one of the first groups to be impacted, we can also become the ones best able to utilize these tools. He also mentioned that we can achieve more by asking AI questions like “How should I try to make this?” I really appreciate his approach to these issues and the advice he offered: taking a more neutral stance when experiencing new things and observing what we can achieve through them.

— Linzhuo, Week 6, after class
