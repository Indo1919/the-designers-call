> I thought this was a really interesting point that connected to both of the excerpts I chose from this week’s readings, especially the idea of looking at the bigger picture and making intentional choices. Karel Martens talks about how designers have to make choices based on the purpose and audience, but there is still uncertainty because every choice creates other possibilities. Juliette’s example made me think about how even something as simple as choosing a colour isn’t necessarily a “right” choice on its own. It depends on the context, the audience, and what you’re trying to communicate. I also saw her points as a good reminder that certain associations aren’t necessarily universal or fixed and often change over time which is important to consider if making design choices based on associations (and maybe not making them based on associations as it won't be very timeless, etc.) Someone also challenged the idea by asking whether choosing not to use blue for a bank to stand out/ differentiate would still be based on the assumption that blue represents trust. Juliette agreed that it is, but said the important thing is being conscious of that association when making the choice. I thought this was interesting because it made me realize that you can’t necessarily avoid existing meanings, you can only be more intentional about how you use or move away from them. This also connected to Erika Hall’s point about looking beyond the immediate user and considering the wider context surrounding a design.
